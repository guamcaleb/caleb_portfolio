The systems behind the gym

A gym needs leads, enrollments, coaching standards and follow-up to run without the owner in the room. I built those pieces for Unified, using AI to write the code. Here is how each one works.

Visit the Unified site

Lead capture and follow-up

An Instagram DM that qualifies and books

People ask about a program in the comments or in a DM. I built the automation that answers right away, asks two questions, and offers a free Test Drive class, so a coach only steps in when someone is ready.

Each offer has its own keyword in ManyChat: the BRIK program, Black Friday early access, three free holiday classes, and coach hiring.

  1. 1

    A keyword starts it

    Someone comments or messages the word BRIK.

  2. 2

    Two quick taps

    The first question asks their goal. The second asks where they are in their training.

    Get strongerBuild my engineTrain for HYROXAll of it
  3. 3

    A plain explanation

    The reply changes with their answers and explains what BRIK is and who it fits.

  4. 4

    One clear offer

    Book a Test Drive, ask a question, or not right now.

  5. 5

    Hand-off and a nudge

    The lead is tagged, a coach is notified and a phone number is captured. A day later, anyone who has not booked gets a short reminder.

The front end

A site where promos switch on and off

unifiedfitguam.com is a single static site with landing pages for each offer: free classes, HYROX, BRIK, Black Friday, and enrollment. The owner switches a page on or off from an admin screen, and the change applies within seconds. A page that is off shows a short message.

That means a seasonal promo ends on schedule without anyone editing code.

Enrollment

Sign a member in person or from their phone

Staff choose the enrollment type, fill in the membership details and lock them. The member then completes their own information, billing authorization and signatures on the front desk device or on their own phone.

Remote enrollments are encrypted. Only the owner can open one.

Coaching

The BASE onboarding program, written down

BASE is a three-day, 1-on-1 onboarding series. I turned it into a playbook coaches can read page by page: Day 1 Squat and Press, Day 2 Hinge and Pull, Day 3 Olympic Lifting. That is 59 pages in all.

The playbook is view only. Anyone who wants a copy fills in a request form and accepts a notice first, and each request is recorded.

How I build with AI

I write the code with Claude, then test it before anything goes live. The same habit applies to the logic behind each decision.

Decision matrix Brand guide survey Skill template

Decision matrix

Score up to five options before you commit

Give each criterion a weight from 1 to 5 for how much it matters. Score each option from 1 to 5 on that criterion. The matrix multiplies, adds up and ranks the options. Then it stress tests the answer by moving every weight up and down one step, and by dropping each criterion in turn, to see whether the leader holds.

Example: where to put a new promo page. Edit anything.

Weighted decision matrix

Weight: 1 nice to have, 2 minor, 3 important, 4 major, 5 must have. Score: 1 poor, 2 weak, 3 okay, 4 good, 5 excellent. A score of 1 on a must-have is flagged. Nothing here is saved or sent.

Brand guide skill

A survey that turns a business into a brand guide

At Unified, the brand guide holds the tagline, four values, the color palette, the voice and five content pillars. Claude reads it before it writes a caption, an email or a landing page, so the output sounds like the gym every time.

This is the survey I use to build that guide for a new business. It also builds the guardrails: the words to avoid, the claims to leave out and the things copy should never mention.

  1. 1

    Send the survey

    The owner answers in their own words and writes "not sure" where it is true.

  2. 2

    Write the guide

    The answers become the tagline, values, voice, word lists, colors, pillars and offers.

  3. 3

    Load it as a Claude skill

    Claude reads the guide before it writes anything.

  4. 4

    Check every draft against it

    When a draft misses, the correction goes back into the guide so the same miss does not repeat.

The basics4 questions
  1. What is the business called, and what does it sell?Answer in one sentence a stranger would understand.
  2. What is the tagline, if there is one?If there is none, finish this sentence: after one visit, people say we are ____.
  3. Why does the business exist?Describe the problem you saw that made you start.
  4. Who is the face of the brand?The owner, the coaches, a character, or no one.
Your customer5 questions
  1. Describe your best customer in three sentences.What they are trying to do matters more than their age.
  2. What do they worry about before they buy?
  3. How do they describe you to a friend?Use their words, not yours. Reviews and DMs are a good source.
  4. Where do they find you?List every place, in order of how often it happens.
  5. Who is not a fit?Describe the customer you would turn away.
Positioning4 questions
  1. What do you do that nearby competitors do not?Name two competitors and say how you differ from each.
  2. What do you refuse to be?Labels, styles or promises you want to stay away from.
  3. What is the one promise you keep every time?
  4. What proof can you cite?Numbers, years, awards, reviews. Say where each one came from.
Values3 questions
  1. Pick three to five values.One word or a short phrase each.
  2. For each value, what does it look like on a normal Tuesday?Describe an action, not an idea.
  3. For each value, what would a customer see or hear that proves it?
Voice and tone7 questions
  1. Pick five words for how you sound.
  2. Pick five words for how you never want to sound.
  3. Paste three real messages that sound like you.Emails, captions or texts you wrote yourself.
  4. Paste one message that sounds wrong, and say why.
  5. How formal are you?Slang, local words, emojis, exclamation points: yes or no, and when.
  6. How does your tone change when someone is upset, when you are promoting something, and when you are teaching?
  7. Do you write short sentences or longer ones?Say the reading level you want.
Words and guardrails7 questions
  1. Which words and phrases do you use all the time?
  2. Which words and phrases do you never use?Include generic industry language you find empty.
  3. What do you call your customers, and what do you call your staff?Members, clients, athletes, guests, coaches, team.
  4. What claims can you not make?Health, legal, pricing, results and comparisons.
  5. What should copy never mention?Things you do not offer, competitors by name, sensitive topics. Write copy about what customers get.
  6. Which topics stay off limits online?Politics, religion, staff conflicts, anything else.
  7. When a fact or number appears in copy, who checks it?
Look and feel5 questions
  1. List your colors with hex codes and what each one is used for.
  2. List your fonts for headlines, body text and small labels.
  3. What are the logo rules?Clear space, minimum size, backgrounds to avoid.
  4. What kind of photos fit?Lighting, people and places. Also say what never fits.
  5. Which shapes, patterns or textures belong to the brand?
Content and offers4 questions
  1. Which three to five themes do you post about again and again?
  2. Which channels do you use, how often, and who runs each one?
  3. What is the main offer?Give the exact name, how the price is worded, and the action you want people to take.
  4. Which calls to action do you use, in order of preference?
Sign-off3 questions
  1. Who approves new work, and how long do they take?
  2. Link three pieces that are exactly on brand and three that are close but wrong.
  3. When AI output misses, how should it be corrected?Edit in place, send back with notes, or rewrite.

Skill build template

A starting point for every new skill

New hires should not start from a blank page. This template gives each Claude skill the same structure: when it runs, what it needs, the steps, the finished format, the brand guardrails and the checks before anything is sent. The owner reviews the same sections every time.

  1. 1

    Pick one repeat task

    Choose something done every week that has a clear finished result.

  2. 2

    Collect real examples

    Gather three approved results and one that missed, so the skill can tell the difference.

  3. 3

    Fill in the template

    Write the steps the way you would explain them to a new hire on day one.

  4. 4

    Test with five real requests

    Include one that should not trigger the skill and one with a missing input.

  5. 5

    Review and log the version

    The owner signs off, fixes the misses and records the version at the bottom of the file.
